APPEARANCE
THE PROBLEM

Good intentions aren't enough.

Most people encounter waste every single day. The challenge is not always a lack of concern. Often, it is the uncertainty of knowing what belongs where and what should happen next.

01 / THE CORE ISSUE

When waste is mixed, everything gets harder.

Different types of waste need different handling. When they are thrown together, the opportunity to recover, recycle, compost, or safely process materials can become more difficult.

WHERE THE CONFUSION STARTS

It looks simple. Until it isn't.

01 / EVERYDAY ITEMS

Not everything is obvious.

A banana peel feels easy to classify. But batteries, chargers, food-stained packaging, mixed-material items, and other everyday objects can leave people unsure.

02 / INFORMATION GAP

The answer isn't always nearby.

Waste rules and disposal options can vary. People may not know where to look for reliable, understandable guidance at the moment they need it.

03 / HABIT

When unsure, people guess.

And when the correct choice feels complicated, the easiest option can become throwing the item into whatever bin is closest.

04 / THE RESULT

Mixed waste creates more work.

Once different waste streams are mixed, sorting, recovery, recycling, composting, and appropriate handling can become more difficult.

WHO IS AFFECTED?

The problem doesn't stop at the bin.

Waste segregation connects everyday decisions to a much larger ecosystem. Different people experience the consequences in different ways.
